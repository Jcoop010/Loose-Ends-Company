The Customer
Follow-Up Starter Kit

A simple, repeatable system for turning missed calls, unsold estimates, quiet leads, and happy customers into the next conversation—and more booked work.

The rule: every open lead has one owner, one next action, and one follow-up date. If any of those are missing, the opportunity is leaking.
01 · Reply fastMissed-call and new-lead scripts that keep the conversation moving.
02 · Recover estimatesPolite follow-ups for quotes that went quiet.
03 · Retain customersCheck-ins, review requests, and win-back messages.
04 · Track the moneyA simple lead tracker and a 15-minute weekly review.

Built for local and service businesses: home services, repair, cleaning, landscaping, salons, studios, consultants, and similar teams. Customize the bracketed fields before sending. Use only channels and contact permissions appropriate for your customers.

1. Set up the system in 30 minutes

  1. Choose one inbox. Use the phone, email, CRM, or shared sheet the team already checks. Avoid creating another place no one opens.
  2. Name one owner. Every lead must have a person responsible for the next action, even if several people can help.
  3. Record the basics. Name, best contact method, service requested, date received, estimate amount if known, status, last contact, next action, and next follow-up date.
  4. Use a simple status set. New · Contacted · Estimate sent · Follow-up due · Booked · Won · Lost · Not a fit.
  5. Schedule two daily sweeps. Check new leads and follow-ups at opening and before close. Block 10 minutes for each.
  6. Close the loop. When someone books, declines, opts out, or is not a fit, update the record so the team does not keep chasing them.

Daily 10-minute closeout checklist

  • Every new lead has been acknowledged.
  • Every open estimate has a next action and date.
  • Every promised callback is assigned.
  • Bookings, lost deals, and opt-outs are recorded.
  • Urgent issues have been escalated to the right person.

2. Ready-to-use message templates

Replace bracketed details. Be truthful about availability, pricing, discounts, and what your business can deliver. One clear question is usually better than a long sales pitch.

Missed call

Template A · Warm and direct
Hi [First Name], this is [Your Name] with [Business]. Sorry we missed your call. What can we help you with? You can reply here or call us at [Number], and we’ll get back to you as soon as we can.

New inquiry received

Template B · Acknowledge and guide
Hi [First Name], thanks for reaching out to [Business] about [Service]. The best next step is [book a time / send a few details / schedule an estimate]. Would [Option 1] or [Option 2] work better for you?

Need more information

Template C · Keep it easy
Hi [First Name], I can help with that. To point you in the right direction, could you share [one or two specific details]? Once I have that, I can explain the best next step.

Estimate follow-up · 2 business days

Template D · Helpful, no pressure
Hi [First Name], I wanted to check that you received the estimate for [Service]. Do you have any questions about the scope, timing, or options? I’m happy to clarify anything that would help you make a decision.

Estimate follow-up · 5–7 days

Template E · Find the blocker
Hi [First Name], just closing the loop on the [Service] estimate. Is this still something you’re considering, or have your plans changed? Either way is completely fine—your reply will help me know how best to follow up.

Availability or scheduling nudge

Template F · Only if accurate
Hi [First Name], if you’d like to move ahead with [Service], I can check our current openings and walk you through the next step. Would you prefer [Option 1] or [Option 2]? I’ll confirm availability before we lock anything in.

Lead went quiet after first reply

Template G · Gentle check-in
Hi [First Name], checking back on your request about [Service]. If you still need help, reply with what works best for you and I’ll point you in the right direction. If now isn’t the right time, no worries.

After booking

Template H · Confirm the next step
You’re all set, [First Name]—we have you scheduled for [Date/Time] for [Service]. [Arrival / preparation detail]. If anything changes or you have a question before then, contact us at [Contact]. Thanks for choosing [Business]!

3. Retention, reviews, and win-back

After a completed job

Template I · Customer care
Hi [First Name], thanks again for choosing [Business] for [Service]. I wanted to make sure everything went smoothly. Is there anything we should address or clarify? We appreciate the chance to help.

Honest review request

Template J · Ask every eligible customer consistently
Hi [First Name], thank you for working with [Business]. If you’re comfortable sharing your experience, an honest review helps other people understand what it’s like to work with us: [Review Link]. We appreciate your feedback either way.

Do not condition incentives on positive reviews, filter requests only to happy customers where platform rules prohibit review gating, or write reviews on a customer’s behalf. Follow the rules of the review platform you use.

Past customer check-in

Template K · Relevant service reminder
Hi [First Name], it’s [Your Name] from [Business]. We helped with [Prior Service] around [Timeframe], and I wanted to check whether you need anything related to [relevant maintenance / seasonal service]. If not, no action needed—just wanted to make it easy to reach us.

Re-engage an old inquiry

Template L · Ask permission to reopen
Hi [First Name], you previously reached out to [Business] about [Service]. I’m checking whether you still need help with it. If you do, I can share current options; if not, I’ll close this out so we don’t keep bothering you.

Responding to a concern

Template M · Acknowledge and resolve
Thanks for letting us know, [First Name]. I’m sorry this hasn’t met your expectations. I’d like to understand what happened and see what we can reasonably do next. Could you share [specific detail] or let me know a good time to speak? I’ll make sure this reaches the right person.

Close the loop

Template N · Respect their time
Hi [First Name], I haven’t heard back, so I’ll close the loop for now rather than keep filling your inbox. If you’d like help with [Service] later, just reply here and we’ll pick it back up. Thanks for considering [Business].

Referral thank-you

Template O · Appreciation
Hi [First Name], thanks for recommending [Business] to [Referral Name / someone you know]. We really appreciate the trust. If they reach out, we’ll take good care of the conversation and explain the next steps clearly.

Five rules that protect trust

  1. Do not invent urgency, scarcity, discounts, guarantees, or appointment slots.
  2. Do not send repeated messages after an opt-out or clear refusal.
  3. Use only the channels the customer reasonably expects and your business is allowed to use.
  4. Keep personal and payment information out of general notes; store only what the team needs.
  5. If a message reveals a complaint, safety concern, refund request, or legal issue, route it to a person who can resolve it.

5. Measure recovered revenue

Track the path from inquiry to outcome. Do not count an estimate as revenue. Count money only when the business confirms the sale or payment according to its own accounting rules.

Lead response rateLeads contacted ÷ new leads received.
Estimate follow-up rateOpen estimates with a logged follow-up ÷ all open estimates.
Booking conversionLeads that booked ÷ qualified leads.
Recovered revenueRevenue actually won from a previously stalled or at-risk opportunity, supported by a record.

Weekly 15-minute review

  • Count new leads, booked jobs, and open estimates.
  • Find any lead with no owner or no next action.
  • Review the oldest five open estimates first.
  • Record which follow-up resulted in a reply, booking, or decline.
  • Calculate confirmed recovered revenue separately from pipeline value.
  • Choose one process improvement for next week.

Start small: load the 10 oldest open estimates first. Contact only where appropriate, log every result, and compare confirmed wins after two weeks. A consistent simple system beats a complicated one no one uses.

One final principle: follow-up is not about pestering people. It is about making the next step easy, keeping promises, and giving customers a reliable way to say yes, no, or not yet.
